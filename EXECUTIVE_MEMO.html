What the testing showed

Live paper trading (real prices, simulated fills) currently stands at a profit factor of 0.374 across 21 trades — the system returned 37 cents for every dollar lost, with a net −8.96%. Historical testing explains why: the core entry signal, tested across 1,850 trades, produced a profit factor of 0.989, which is a coin flip before costs. No strategy reached statistical significance; the best p-value was 0.117 against a required 0.05.

The six gates that protected the capital

Gates 4 and 5 must be satisfied together. A strong profit factor across a handful of trades can arise from luck; the significance test asks whether a result that good could have occurred by chance. Requiring both is what stopped an earlier candidate — which looked convincing — from reaching live capital.

The 435-pair universe screen

Before concluding, 31 instruments were screened across FX, equity indices, commodities and crypto, covering 2018–2026 — 435 instrument pairs in total.

The statistical correction is the finding. Testing 435 pairs at the conventional 5% threshold produces roughly 22 false positives by arithmetic alone. Applying Benjamini-Hochberg (false discovery rate) and Bonferroni corrections:

Measure Result
Raw "significant" results at p < 0.05 37
Expected from chance alone 21.8
Survive strict correction 2
Tradeable candidates 0

Both survivors pair market volatility (VIX) against gold and silver. They are genuinely related over the long run, but the spread between them moves randomly — so there is no reliable point at which it returns, and therefore nothing to trade. Every other instrument measured as statistically indistinguishable from random price movement.

This also corrected an earlier error in our own work: a smaller screen found three "significant" pairs where chance predicts 3.3, and one was advanced for development before failing outright. That is precisely the mistake this corrected screen is designed to prevent.

Next steps: a change of inputs, not of settings

Further adjustment of retail technical patterns has been stopped. Repeatedly tuning the same indicators against the same price history produces results that look strong and do not survive contact with new data — a pattern we observed directly and repeatedly.

Research has moved to genuine quantitative inputs:

  • Macro drivers — the US dollar index and inflation-adjusted Treasury yields, being gold's established fundamental drivers, rather than chart shapes.
  • Statistical pre-screening — instruments are now tested for exploitable structure before any strategy is designed for them, with multiple-comparison correction applied as standard.
  • Honest cost accounting — transaction costs measured against 204,214 real market ticks, replacing an assumed figure that understated them.

One genuine result carried forward: the macro filter does not create profit, but it reduced maximum drawdown from 33% to 12.4%. It is a demonstrable risk control.

Commitment

Our present assessment is that the system as it stands is unlikely to meet these gates. We would rather tell you that now than discover it with your capital at risk.
